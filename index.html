ROC Curve Lab

A spam filter gives every email a score, and you choose the score where it starts flagging. Each choice produces a different mix of right and wrong calls. This page turns that mix into the four rates (TPR, TNR, FNR, FPR) and then into a ROC curve.

Rows are the truth and columns are the filter's call. scikit-learn's confusion_matrix lists the negative class first, so it returns [[TN, FP], [FN, TP]]. The four numbers are the same, only the order differs.

Read each name in two parts. The first word says whether the filter was right (true) or wrong (false). The second says what the filter said (positive is spam, negative is ham). A rate then divides by everyone who really belongs to that row.

TPR
True positive rate
TP / (TP + FN)

Of all real spam, the share the filter caught.

Also called sensitivity, recall, hit rate

FNR
False negative rate
FN / (TP + FN)

Of all real spam, the share that slipped into the inbox.

Also called miss rate

FPR
False positive rate
FP / (FP + TN)

Of all real ham, the share wrongly flagged as spam.

Also called fall-out, 1 − specificity

TNR
True negative rate
TN / (TN + FP)

Of all real ham, the share correctly left alone.

Also called specificity

The two rates in each row always add to 1, because every real spam email is either caught or missed, and every real ham email is either flagged or left alone. A ROC curve plots TPR against FPR, which is how often the filter flags spam compared with how often it flags ham.
